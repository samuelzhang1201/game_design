# Crown and Council

Text-first, LLM-narrated grand strategy game: rule a kingdom for ~60 years through a council of ministers with real personalities, ideologies, and grudges. Godot 4 project with a working 2D map; the next major track is the **3D campaign map** (Terrain3D, `tools/map3d/README.md`).

## Key documents

- `docs/design/PRD.md` — scope of the first playable build. Build order lives here; follow it.
- `docs/design/COUNCIL_SYSTEM.md` — the council design doc; source of truth for mechanics and formulas.
- `docs/design/MANOR_SYSTEM.md` — the manor system (owner 2026-09-13): every land cell of the pack is a **manor** with a proper name (`political/manors.csv`, forged by `NameForge` / `ManorNames` from the export's name banks; `manor_name_overrides.csv` is hand-maintained) held by a **house** (a family name; the crown is the player character's live family; an unmoved manor belongs to its province's house, so `GameState.manor_owner` holds only what moved and `MapManors.set_owner` needs a reason — `manor_log`). People are never stored and are WHOLE: a province's ledger population is apportioned to its manors by area × land value (largest remainder, `MapManors.people_table`), so manors always sum EXACTLY to their province. **The economy is `ManorEconomy` and `RealmEconomy`, not `MapManors`**: `ManorEconomy.line_of` is one manor's MONTH — whole people, whole-gold tax (its town's tariff included), the units of goods it ships and their worth in the strategic and civil pools, THE ONLY ROUNDING IN THE ECONOMY (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-economy-and-staging-design.md`) — and `RealmEconomy` sums those lines into provinces and the realm (columnar, a key per province standing in for a dirty flag, the month's work spread over balanced day buckets; `line_of` / `lines_of_province` / `lines_table` / `tax_table` read the cache, one province key check per bulk read, never one per row), settled at the month's end into `metrics.treasury`, `metrics.strategic` and `metrics.grain` — the CIVIL pool, whose key stays `grain` for its readers. Buildings (`data/manor_buildings.csv`, priced in civil goods by `civil_cost`; `cost_gold` is unused), defence, value (whole people and the month's units, `MapManors.value_of_line`) and the holding house's asking price (marked up by its councillor's opinion) are `MapManors`, every number `balance.csv` `manor_*`. Orders `MANOR_BUY` / `MANOR_SELL` / `MANOR_BUILD` / `MANOR_SEIZE` (a skeleton gated by `manor_seize_enabled`) resolve a manor by code, label or id (`MapManors.find`) and run from the manor card's buttons through `Game.try_execute`. `world_cell_index.png` (importer / `tools/bake_manors.gd`) is how a click finds its manor — only in the ECONOMY and MANORS layers; on the political map land still means province. The **MANORS** tab colours manors by house (`MapPalette.house_color`), puts the **Manor Master** table in the left column, tints the town drawings with their manor's colour (`sprite_tint`, `map_manor_mark_alpha`), and draws ONE **estate** per manor without a town by its INDUSTRY and its people (owner 2026-09-14; library `D-43`..`D-78`: twelve industries — the chief goods of `manor_focus.csv` — times three sizes, each drawing the house with the industry's workplace, grain with windmills, pasture with herds, a quay for fish, salt pans, a quarry, a mine; installed by `install_map_art.py estates` into `assets/manors/<focus>_<size>.png`, greyed and lightened like the settlements): `data/manor_estates.csv` gives each industry's three `min_people` (the pack's manor-population terciles); `MapObjects.estate_tier(repo, focus, people)` picks the row (an industry with no art wears the first industry's), `MapObjects.manor_estates` centres the drawing on the cell at ONE height for all (`map_manor_estate_px`, capped by `map_manor_estate_fit` of the cell radius — owner: no big and small), mirrored by hash; the manor card's "Estate" field names industry and size; `WorldMapView.estate_mesh` is ONE `MarkMesh` tinted per manor, rebuilt when the ledger's people, a chosen focus or an owner changes — so a manor that grows or is turned to another good changes its drawing on its own. The earlier procedural farmsteads-and-fields marks were REMOVED at the owner's ruling (scattered and unreasonable): never place separate houses and fields by hash again; the estate is one drawing. The default map stays as it was. **The player is the king of the realm and the lord of ONE province** (owner 2026-09-13): a province is controlled by the house holding the most of its manors (`MapManors.controller_of`, ties to the province's own house), the crown starts controlling the capital's province alone (`house_bindings.csv`), and everything that reads as the crown's economy — the seeded lord's purse, the dashboard's people and provinces, the ECONOMY column, `WorldLedger.belongs_to_player`, the order prompt's "Your provinces" — is `MapManors.controlled_provinces`; the host stays the realm's; BUILD and FOCUS need a controlled province, SEIZE any province of the realm. **Two purses** (owner 2026-09-14, MANOR_SYSTEM.md §5c): the CROWN's `treasury`/`strategic`/`grain` (a crown in the top bar) take `crown_share_permille` of the realm's other provinces and pay the court and the orders' fees; the LORD's `lord_gold`/`lord_strategic`/`lord_civil` (the house's arms) take the controlled provinces in full, the harvest and the buildings, and pay every manor order inside the realm — `RealmEconomy.purses` is the one split. **Buying land** (`MapManors.purchase_kind`): your own province or a realm province bordering one, from the lord's purse; across the border only a manor whose cell touches the realm, from the treasury, annexed (`MapManors.annex`, `GameState.manor_province`, saved) into the bordering realm province with the most tax and held by its house; anything else refused. A manor's province is therefore STATE: `province_of` / `manors_of_province` take the state, and `MapOwnership.province_loops` merges annexed cells into the outlines by edge parity, so borders, seams, wash and `RealmEconomy`'s index follow. Every manor works one **chief good** (`data/manor_focus.csv`, allowed by biome and coast; the default FOLLOWS THE TERRAIN — owner 2026-09-14 — the first row whose `default_on` claims the cell (`coast`, `high`/`peak` by `manor_focus_*_height`, or biome ids) and that the land allows, else the first allowed; `GameState.manor_focus` holds only chosen ones with the tick, ramping in over `transition_ticks`): yield is people × land value × `yield_per_1000` × the good's growth, whole units a MONTH (`ManorEconomy.focus_units`), it counts in the value, and joins the ECONOMY column through `MapEconomy.output_of_provinces(repo, provinces, state)`, which sums the same lines the tick settles; `MANOR_FOCUS` runs from the card's `WORK` buttons like the other manor orders — no model call. **Time runs in days** (owner 2026-09-13): `GameState.day` (1..30) under the tick, the top bar's clock (`||` `>` `2x` `4x`, `time_day_seconds`, `time_speed_*`) driven only by `GameRoot._process` on the court AND the council stage (owner 2026-09-14: play is the only switch — no Wave them on first) through `Game.advance_day` — works step each day, the thirtieth day's end is the tick, an event stops the clock and opens the council, and there is no End Turn (owner 2026-09-14: play is the only switch); no wall-clock below `Game`. **Building takes time and room**: `build_days` per building, works in `GameState.manor_works`, `manor_slots_base` (+`manor_slots_burg_bonus`) slots per manor, `MANOR_DEMOLISH` refunds `manor_demolish_refund` of a building's `civil_cost` to the CIVIL STORES (a build is bought with goods, so goods come back); finished buildings add `*_per_tick` (gold, grain, church, contentment, unrest) as ledger lines after the month's settlement and `produce_good/units` to the ECONOMY column; the card's slot row (`MapDetailPanel._fill_slots`, icons `bld_*` in `resource_icons.csv`) carries a cross per building.

Exception: the map pack tables under `data/maps/<id>/` — `map_info.csv`, `realm_bindings.csv`, and `house_bindings.csv` (both bindings hand-maintained, never written by the importer: realms bind authored slots to kingdoms, houses bind the court's families to provinces of the player's realm; unbound provinces are held by a house named after their seat) at the root and, by domain, `geography/` (`biomes`, `rivers`, `markers`, `routes`), `political/` (`kingdoms`, `provinces`, `burgs`, `borders`, `province_borders`, `diplomacy`, `campaigns`), `economy/` (`kingdoms`, `provinces`, `burgs`, `goods`, `markets`, `market_goods`, `production`), `military/` (`kingdoms`, `regiments`, `unit_types`), `society/` (`cultures`, `religions`, `zones`); `data/` carries a `.gdignore` at its root (the per-domain ones are now redundant but harmless) so Godot does not import game tables as translations — it was generating ~120 `.translation`/`.csv.import` sidecars nothing loads; `FileAccess` reads the CSVs regardless, and a future export preset must include `data/**` explicitly — plus the `world_state_index.png` / `world_province_index.png` / `world_biome_index.png` hit-test bitmaps are **generated** by `tools/import_azgaar.gd` from the Azgaar JSON export in `assets/maps/<id>/` — hand-edits are overwritten on re-import; lasting changes belong in Azgaar. **There is no map render PNG** (owner ruling 2026-09-11): the terrain is drawn by the game from the pack's cells — `geography/vertices.csv`, `cells.csv` (vertex ids, height, biome, water kind, neighbours) and `coasts.csv` (land and lake shores) — through `MapTerrain`, one vertex-coloured mesh whose colours come from `data/terrain_palette.csv` (a land tint per biome, sea, lake, and line colours) and whose relief and stroke widths come from `balance.csv` (`map_relief_*`, `map_light_*`, `map_river_*`, ...). The sheet is parchment (owner ruling 2026-09-12): `terrain_palette.csv` is one narrow warm-brown scale, the sea is the same sheet greyed a step, and `WorldMapView` lays a seamless `NoiseTexture2D` grain and a radial vignette over it in screen px (`map_paper_*`) — the map is printed ON the paper, so the paper does not zoom. **There are no coloured country areas**: `map_political_alpha` is 0, so `_draw_political_wash` returns early; ownership reads from a **bold dashed** kingdom border and **thin dashed** province seams, both stepped along the line's own arc length by `MapGeometry.dash_polyline` / `dash_segments` (Godot's `draw_dashed_line` restarts its phase per call, which turned the B-spline rings to mush). **Every dashed line is cut ONCE per zoom, never per frame** (2026-09-13, after `tools/bench_map.gd` found the per-frame cut and its one-command-per-dash drawing holding a pan at 4–9 fps): `MapDashCache` (`scripts/engine/`, pure, tested) keeps each line set — a kingdom's rings, a realm's seams, every lane — cut in NATIVE px for the current zoom as ONE polyline whose vertices carry a phase (dash / gap / an invisible jump between lines), thinned to `map_dash_min_step_px`; a frame transforms it with one `Transform2D * PackedVector2Array` and draws it with one `draw_polyline_colors`, the phases coloured ink / pale (the chequered border) or ink / clear (seams, lanes). A zoom or an ownership change empties the cache. What is left per frame is the strip builder's cost per vertex. Never go back to `draw_multiline` for a dashed layer: with a width above 1 px it is one canvas command per pair. The bench went from 4–9 fps to 28–87 fps on a pan. **The terrain decor is ONE mesh over ONE atlas** (2026-09-13, the decor rework the owner approved before filling the map with hamlets, fields and building icons): `MarkAtlas` (`scripts/engine/`, pure) shelf-packs the terrain art into one padded sheet with mipmaps at load, `MarkMesh` turns a list of marks `{pos, texture, height, color}` into one `ArrayMesh` of standing quads in NATIVE px (y-sorted, tint as vertex colour), and `WorldMapView._draw_terrain_decor` is a single `draw_mesh` whose `Transform2D` is the view's offset and zoom — rebuilt only when ownership changes (`decor_mesh()` keyed on `_ownership_sig`), never per frame or per zoom; the GPU clips what is off screen. That took the political pan from ~29 ms (decor 7.5 ms of `_draw` plus ~10 ms of the renderer's canvas-command CPU) to the heights view's ~10 ms. Every new layer of marks — hamlets per manor, field patterns per chief good, building icons — goes through the same two classes: never one `draw_texture_rect` per mark again. `terrain_sprites()` remains as a test seam only. `MapPalette` and the wash code stay in place — putting the colour back is one number in `balance.csv`, no code — and `MapPalette.color_of` still tints the country card, the map detail panel and the military bars. `WorldMapView` draws the coasts, rivers, routes, every kingdom's border, the province seams, the labels and a scale bar itself, so conquest redraws with no new art. Rivers are smoothed once per course (`MapGeometry.smooth_open`, cached — the pack stores raw polylines whose corners read as kinks), **in the pack's own order, which IS source → mouth** (Azgaar writes a river's cells that way and the importer keeps them; the earlier "the end nearer the coast is the mouth" guess turned short coastal rivers round, owner 2026-09-13), **clipped to the land at both ends with each cut bisected to the shoreline** (`_trim_to_land` / `_shore_between`: Azgaar runs a mouth one cell out to sea, and a river must begin where the sea ends, never in it), and drawn as a **solid tapered ribbon** (owner 2026-09-13) — `MapGeometry.ribbon` builds a triangle band whose half-width varies per point, because `draw_polyline` takes one width for the whole line. The river is **a point at its source and widest at its mouth**, swelling as `t^map_river_taper_gamma` (`WorldMapView.river_taper`; owner 2026-09-13, reversing the same morning's ruling); no caps — the mouth's flat cut lies on the shoreline. There is no soft halo band, and `map_river_alpha` is 1 — below 1 the ribbon's overlapping triangles on tight bends show as blots. Its COLOUR belongs to the sea (`river` `#7FB8DD`, owner 2026-09-17: 太蓝了 — a river and the mouth it runs into must read as ONE water), one step deeper than `sea_far` so a tapered ribbon still shows over the pale land; `#1E6FB5` was the deep blue. **Roads are worn sand tracks, never ruled lines** (owner 2026-09-17, after the same painted reference the settlements came from): `road` `#C2A981` and `trail` `#D0BC99` — pale warm earth — at `map_road_width` 2.4 under a soft shoulder (`map_road_soft_ratio` 0.6 × `map_road_soft_alpha`), so a road reads by its WIDTH and its fade into the ground instead of as the slate line `#4B5563` drawn across the sheet. **The sea is four flat bands by distance from the shore**, coloured by SCHEME C since the ground became a photograph (owner 2026-09-17: a green shallow shelf `sea_near` #A8C4B4 going out through #6FA3A0 and #3D7A93 to a deep indigo `sea_deep` #255C7C, with `coast_line` #1E4257 — the old flat bright blue read as cut paper against photographic land) (owner 2026-09-13, after the reference chart; the same day's hatching rings are GONE — owner: completely wrong): `SeaLayer`, a Node2D child of the view drawn `show_behind_parent`, paints ONE quad through `sea_bands.gdshader`, which samples the pack's `world_sea_distance.png` — every pixel its distance to land in native px, baked by `MapIndex.bake_sea_distance` in the importer (`tools/bake_sea_distance.gd` bakes it for a pack imported earlier; a test asserts it exists at the canvas size) — into `sea_near` / `sea_nearer` / `sea_far` / `sea_deep` at `map_sea_band_*_px`, each edge a `map_sea_band_soft_px` fade, over a fixed simplex wave grain (`map_sea_wave_*`, part of the drawing, so it zooms; its seed is data). **Sea cells are NOT in the terrain mesh** any more (lakes are), so the land mesh draws over the sea and the shore is the land's own edge; the view sets the layer's position and scale to its offset and zoom each frame. **A compass rose** (library M-13, drawn as vectors in the scale bar's ink) is fixed in the top-right corner, `map_compass_*`. **Ships and a whale are printed on the open sea**: `data/sea_art.csv` rows placed by `MapObjects.sea_decor` over the sea cells by AREA (one candidate per `map_sea_decor_spacing_px` square, hashed from the cell id — the pack's sea cells are few and huge, never more than two hops from land, so nothing is per cell), thinned by `map_sea_decor_density`, kept `min_shore_px` off the land coast and `map_sea_decor_min_gap_px` apart from each other — never overlapping (no seed, no state), tinted `sea_ink` at `map_sea_decor_alpha`, art from `assets/decor/` (library D-19 carrack, D-20 cog, D-22 whale — no monsters, owner 2026-09-12); a missing PNG simply draws nothing. The scale bar is bottom-right, over the vignette, and restates itself at every zoom: the pack's own `distance_scale` / `distance_unit` from `map_info.csv`, rounded down to the 1-2-5 ladder by `MapGeometry.nice_step` so it reads "200 km" and never "187 km". Settlement art draws at its tier's **share** of the largest drawing's height — the `size` column of `data/settlement_art.csv`, capital 1.0 down to hamlet 0.25 — never at the PNG's own pixel height, which made the hierarchy untunable without regenerating art. Only a **clicked** object is ringed; hovering names it in the tooltip and swaps the left column but draws nothing on the map (owner 2026-09-12). All pack coordinates are the JSON's own (2560×929 for `beria`); nothing is calibrated or scaled at import. The importer needs only the `*Full*.json`; it never reads a PNG.

## Stack

- **Godot 4.x + GDScript** (typed GDScript everywhere: type hints on vars, params, returns).
- **A tick is a MONTH of 30 days** (`Calendar.DAYS_PER_TICK`, 12 ticks a year): the clock runs `GameState.day` 1..30 under the tick, and the month's end is the settlement.
- **GUT** (Godot Unit Test) for tests. The simulation engine must be unit-testable headless.
- **OpenAI API via raw HTTP** (`HTTPRequest` node) — there is no GDScript SDK. Model IDs and prices live in one config table (see PRD §8), not scattered through code.
- **Run:** `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path .`
- **Test:** `./run_tests.sh` (runs the full GUT suite headless; the suite must report the **expected error count** — any other count is fatal. That is "Errors 2" while the `gdai-mcp-plugin-godot` GExtension is installed, whose class-database probing GUT cannot resolve headless, and 0 without it; the script keys off `gdai_mcp_plugin.gdextension`, which is git-ignored along with `bin/`, so a fresh clone expects 0 until the binaries are copied in from the plugin zip). `run_tests.sh` exports `CC_FORCE_OFFLINE=1` so the suite can never make a live API call even with a real `.env` present.
- **`.env`** (git-ignored, dev-convenience source for `OpenAIClient.load_config()`; `.env.example` documents the keys — `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `LLM_MODEL`/per-tier overrides, `LLM_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS`) overlays defaults and is itself overlaid by `user://config.json` (player override); a missing key or `CC_FORCE_OFFLINE=1` fails closed to `offline_mode: true`.
- **`user://config.json`** (git-ignored, read by `OpenAIClient.load_config()`; missing/unreadable/malformed file, or an empty `api_key`, all fail closed to `offline_mode: true`):
  ```json
  {
    "api_key": "",
    "model_parse": "gpt-5.6-luna",
    "model_voice": "gpt-5.6-terra",
    "offline_mode": true
  }
  ```

## Architecture invariants (do not violate)

1. **The engine decides; the LLM only writes dialogue.** Concern scans, advisor suggestions, opinion drift, faction power, and escalation are all deterministic engine code. A minister's suggestion is a real `action_code` with real params — always legal, always affordable. Never let the model invent actions. The model may also propose **world deltas** (owner ruling 2026-09-09), but only on paths listed in `data/world_schema.csv`, each clamped to that path's `llm_cap`, each carrying a reason, and only on the player's own realm; the engine still picks the speaker and applies everything through `WorldLedger`.
2. **Determinism.** One seeded RNG stream owned by the engine. No `Time.get_*`, no wall-clock, no unseeded `randi()` inside simulation code. Identical save + identical inputs ⇒ identical state. LLM output enters the engine only as validated action codes, clamped enums (e.g. `tone` deltas capped at ±3), and schema-listed world deltas clamped to their caps.
3. **Simulation core is engine-pure.** Keep sim classes as `RefCounted` (no Node/scene dependencies) so GUT can run them headless. UI observes state via signals; it never mutates it directly.
4. **Readable causality.** Every opinion change carries a reason string at creation time (`−8: revoked noble tax exemption (opposes, centralism)`). Never write an opinion delta without one.
5. **Authored prose never names a kingdom.** The map is a generated Azgaar
   export, so story text, fact text, role names and templates say
   `{player_realm}` / `{raider_realm}` / `{neighbor_realm}` / `{empire_realm}`;
   `DataRepository.fill_realms()` substitutes the bound kingdom's label at
   load. Structural columns keep using the slot itself (`resolve_realm`).
   A re-export that renames the world should touch
   `data/maps/<id>/realm_bindings.csv` and nothing else — tests included:
   fixtures derive their kingdom, capital and roads from the pack, never
   from a literal name.
6. **Territory is state, not pack data.** The map pack ships the *starting*
   border; who holds a province now lives in `GameState.province_owner`
   (only what has moved) and moves through `MapOwnership.set_owner`, which
   requires a reason like every other state change. A kingdom's live outline
   is the merge of the loops of the provinces it holds
   (`province_borders.csv`), so conquest redraws the border, recentres the
   label and repaints the land with no new art. Hit-testing reads the
   province index bitmap — geometry, which never goes stale — and looks the
   owner up; never trust `world_state_index.png` for ownership.
7. **Save = one JSON file.** All state in memory, serialized whole. No database, no partial saves.
8. **Every order carries a cost.** `OrderCosts` guarantees an explicit (gold, grain, population) or hidden (opinion, relationship, attribute) cost per executed order from `data/order_costs.csv` (polarity per metric in `data/metric_polarity.csv`); `COUNCIL_CONSULT` and `EVENT_CHOICE` are the listed exemptions. A typed order is ONE model call returning `{narrative, impacts}`; the screens print the narrative and then the ledger lines that actually applied, so a narrative that overreaches is visible as such.
9. **Cost discipline.** Clicked suggestions bypass the LLM entirely. Low-stakes minister lines use trait-tinted templates with no API call. Reserve the flagship model for rare confrontation/betrayal scenes only.
10. **The columns divide by kind** (owner ruling 2026-09-10). The LEFT
    column is FACTS: the realm dashboard, the country under the cursor, the
    host while the military layer is on, and the fact card of whatever the
    player clicks on the map — clicking an object replaces the left column,
    never the right. The RIGHT slot is PEOPLE: the council column — where
    you stand, who is present, MOVE, and the full record of what was said
    (`ScreenLayout.TALK_WIDTH` on the conversation stage). The TALK itself
    is the **audience window** (owner 2026-09-15, after Crusader Kings III,
    replacing the dossier over the talk in the right column of 2026-09-10):
    centred over the map (`%AudienceLayer` lies exactly on the map panel,
    faintly dimming it), the person on the left — portrait cut head to
    waist before the room (`AudienceBackdrop`: the location kind's tint, or
    a `data/locations.csv` `backdrop` painting), name plate, seat, opinion
    whose tooltip lists its last reasons (`CharacterPortrait.opinion_summary`),
    trait chips with their notes, CK3's row of five skill badges — and on
    the right this beat's exchange (`speech_text`; the record keeps every
    beat), BIO / SKILLS / TIES pages that take the speech's place and hand
    it back, the options (their trade-off and gold cost on hover) and the
    talk box. Events and the opening narration stand in the SAME window
    (`event_popup()` is it); only the MOVE list keeps the small popup. So a
    map click never displaces the talk, and the map only narrows for the
    council column.
    The left column's width is fixed
    (`ScreenLayout.LEFT_WIDTH`, 120 design px): anything put in it must wrap
    — a label that cannot wrap sets a minimum width and pushes the column
    over the map — and scroll rather than grow. Each screen owns one
    `_refresh_left()` that applies the precedence (card > hovered country >
    military > the column's own content); no panel hides its own siblings.
11. **The economy is whole gold, rounded once, at the manor.**
    Provinces and the realm are sums; growth is an integer per-mille
    table; iteration order is fixed.

**Settlements are AERIAL PHOTOGRAPHS, like the ground** (owner 2026-09-17, replacing the painted illustrations of 2026-09-16 once the ground became satellite: a painted town on a photographed field read as a sticker). `assets/flux/town_prompts_sat.csv` + `STYLE_CORE_town_satblend.txt` draw each tier straight down from a survey aircraft in the same daylight as the fields, and — the part prompts alone could never fix — the built area **THINS OUTWARD** into farmsteads, hedgerows and strip fields until nothing is left, with no wall ring, no ground slab and no cast shadow. Install by fitting to the tier's height into `assets/settlements/painted/<tier>.png` and `_01`/`_02` — **a cut-out under 15% coverage is refused**, and `data/settlement_art.csv` `variants` is 2 (the old painted `_03`..`_10` variants were deleted in the 2026-09-18 cleanup; raising it needs new aerial variants first). They are drawn PURELY PROPORTIONAL: `map_sprite_scale` 0.12 with `map_sprite_max_px` 400, which is out of reach at every zoom the view allows — at 110 the closest zoom clipped a 125 px ask, so towns stopped growing with the camera and read as shrinking against the map. `map_sprite_tint` is 0.12 and `map_sprite_alpha` 1.0: a photographed town needs almost none of the realm's colour. **Do not rebuild these as procedural 3D.** The Blender route ran five rounds and was rejected as too crude (owner 2026-09-16: "3d城市的建模没有用，删掉"); at ~80 px per house what reads is geometry density, not texture relief.

**Map symbol art is GREYSCALE on purpose.** Everything in `assets/terrain/` (and, before 2026-09-15, `assets/settlements/`) is drawn through a multiply tint of the realm that holds its province (`WorldMapView.realm_tint`), so the art carries only VALUE and the hue comes from `political_palette.csv`. Sepia art times a realm colour stays sepia — the brown wins and the parchment shows through the coloured washes; grey times a realm colour IS that colour. Every mark — terrain and settlement alike — is also lightened 45% toward the paper IN THE FILE rather than drawn translucent: a translucent mark cannot occlude the one under it, and a wood is a clump of overlapping marks. One fade value across the whole sheet, so nothing sits at a different depth from its neighbours. Province is fixed geometry (resolved once from the province index bitmap); OWNER is looked up live, which is what makes conquest recolour a realm's towns and woods with no new art.

**THE GROUND IS A SATELLITE PHOTOGRAPH** (owner 2026-09-17, replacing the colour-pencil biome tiles of 2026-09-14: "我确实喜欢卫星版本"). The land of the default map wears a true-colour aerial texture per biome — `assets/flux/terrain_prompts_v2.csv` + `STYLE_CORE_satellite_flat.txt`, installed by `install_map_art.py biomes` at **1024 px** (`BIOME_TILE_PX`; 512 threw away half of the render and magnified 3.7x at the closest zoom) into `assets/biomes/<key>.png`, mapped by `data/biome_art.csv`. A tile carries MATERIAL ONLY — rock, scree, snow, turf, hedged fields — and **no landform**: shape words ("sharp ridgelines", "hachure on the slopes") plus a seamless canvas degenerate into a wallpaper of vertical feathers, so the relief comes from the map's own height instead. **Mountains are HEIGHT, not a biome**: `MapIndex.raise_high_ground` rewrites a land pixel's biome id to 13 hill / 14 mountain / 15 peak by its cell's height (`map_biome_hill_height` 45 / `map_biome_mountain_height` 62 / `map_biome_peak_height` 72 — at 80 the snow was scattered specks, at 72 the peaks join into a range; dropping the mountain line with it spreads bare rock around the snow and reads dirty), so the existing weight bake blends high ground in like any other ground and the shader needs no second path. `MapIndex.bake_relief` lays each cell's height over its pixels as an L8 field. **Both are baked by `tools/bake_relief.gd`, each with its OWN resolution and blur** (`map_relief_scale` 2 / `map_relief_blur_px` 4; `map_biome_weights_scale` 2): the weight maps want a wide blur to cross-fade tiles, a SLOPE wants the landforms — baked at the tiles' 1/4 and radius 3 the field's wavelength was hundreds of native px and the hillshade read as half the sheet bright, half dark. `BiomeLayer` (`scripts/ui/`, a child of `WorldMapView` behind it like `SeaLayer`) draws the land mesh through `biome_tiles.gdshader`, which mixes **sixteen** layers by `world_biome_weights_0..3.png` and lights them by the height field's own normal — **classic hillshade**, flat ground normalised to 1.0, `map_relief_gain` 16 the vertical exaggeration and `map_relief_shade` 0.20 how far a lit or shaded face departs from flat (the first form multiplied the light by `clamp(length(slope) * gain)` and fell into a saturation trap: nothing below a gain of ~10, dirty patches above, no usable range between). **TILE LOD** (owner 2026-09-17, after "zoom in 之后好模糊" and then a chequerboard at world zoom): one tile size cannot serve both ends — 128 native px is a 0.92x minification up close (sharp) but repeats 13.6x across the world view, 384 is clean far but a 2.75x magnification near — so the shader samples the same tile at `map_biome_tile_px` 128 and `map_biome_tile_px_far` 384 and crossfades by the view's zoom between `map_biome_lod_far_zoom` 1.8 and `map_biome_lod_near_zoom` 4.0, fed live by `BiomeLayer.set_view_zoom` from `WorldMapView`. **The terrain SYMBOLS are off** (`map_decor_alpha` 0, owner 2026-09-17: the photograph already draws the woods); `MapObjects.terrain_decor`, `MarkAtlas` and `MarkMesh` stay for the MANORS layer and for putting them back. Re-run `tools/bake_relief.gd` after changing any band, scale or blur.

**The game must look right at ANY window size, not just integer multiples of the design viewport.** `rendering/textures/canvas_textures/default_texture_filter` is **Linear with mipmaps** and every art `.import` generates mipmaps (the three `*_index.png` hit-test bitmaps are the exception — they are data read as `Image`, never sampled). Nearest is only crisp at an exact integer scale; on a 1512x982 or 2560x1600 display — most real monitors — it point-samples every texture and the screen reads as ragged. Mipmaps matter as much on the way down: a 256 px terrain symbol is drawn at ~20 design px. Fonts need nothing: Godot re-rasterises them at the window resolution under `canvas_items` stretch, so they are sharp at every ratio. `tools/snapshot_map.gd --size N` renders the map at N x N*9/16 instead of the design viewport, for looking at the ground's sharpness at full resolution. **`tools/snapshot_screen.gd --resolution WxH` renders through the REAL window** and is the only faithful way to check this; its `--scale` mode uses a SubViewport and a scaled Control, which looks similar and exercises a different path.

**The design viewport is 1280x720** (raised from 640x360 on 2026-09-12). `project.godot` stretches with `canvas_items`, so the game lays out in design units and draws at the window resolution; at the default 1280x720 window that is now 1 real pixel per design pixel, which is the whole point — a raster drawn into an N-design-px box gets N real pixels to work with instead of N/2. Every design-px number was doubled with it: `balance.csv`'s screen-px keys and the thresholds compared against `view_scale()` (which doubles for the same framing), the `const`s in `scripts/ui`, every font size, and the literals in the drawing code. Three kinds of number were deliberately LEFT ALONE, and the same test applies to anything added later: values already multiplied by `view_scale()` (`map_sprite_scale`, `map_label_scale_px`, `map_river_discharge_scale`), values in some texture's OWN pixels (`MAP_EDGE_CROP`, `PANEL_ART_SOURCE_INSET`, `ResourceIcon`'s `u := s / 9.0` glyph grid), and values in native map px (`PREVIEW_SPAN`). `tests/ui/test_display_fit.gd` pins the viewport.

**The UI is a DARK theme after Crusader Kings III** (owner 2026-09-15, reversing the bright modern theme of 2026-09-13; the map keeps its look): `UiPalette` holds light parchment ink (`TEXT_SOFT`) on dark leather cards (`PANEL_BG`) with cut corners and a deep shadow, a gold accent (`GOLD` is gold again) and trim, dark buttons (`button_looks`: `CHIP_BG` / `CHIP_HOVER` / `CHIP_PRESSED`), sunk inputs (`INPUT_BG`), dark tooltips, separators and scroll bars, and CK3 option bars (`option_button`); each constant keeps its bright predecessor in a comment, and `tests/ui/test_ui_palette_dark.gd` pins the contrast rather than the hexes. The panel ornament is dark ink on clear, so `VellumPanelStyle.tint` draws it in `ORNAMENT_TINT`. `chart_palette.csv`, `LocationView.KIND_COLORS`, the speaker names and the news glass were retuned to read on the dark. The bright note follows for reference: `UiPalette` held dark slate ink on near-white rounded cards with a soft shadow, a royal-blue accent and gold trim. `ui_panel_art` is 0, so the vellum plate below is off; the map's land, sea and realm washes were brightened in `terrain_palette.csv` / `political_palette.csv`, and the paper grain is a trace with no vignette. **Fonts divide by job** (owner 2026-09-13, all OFL, licenses in `assets/fonts/`): **UnifrakturMaguntia** for the game title, headers (`UiPalette.style_header`, drawn `TITLE_SIZE_SCALE` larger because blackletter reads small; ALL-CAPS header text is set in title case by `readable_title`, since blackletter capitals in a row cannot be read) and the **kingdom names on the map** (`map_label_font`, drawn in the name's own case with `map_label_tracking` near 0); **Cinzel Decorative** on tab buttons (`style_tab`); **Cinzel** for everything else — dialogue, options, long text — as the theme default and the transcript's RichTextLabel fonts (the file is one weight and has no italic, so bold is an emboldened and italic a slanted `FontVariation`), plus the scale bar and the heraldic shield (`display_font`). The transcript's ink is set on RichTextLabel's own `default_color` — it does not inherit the Label colour, and unset it drew white. ## Conventions

- GDScript style: `snake_case` files/functions/vars, `PascalCase` classes, signals named in past tense (`opinion_changed`).
- The API key is read from a local git-ignored config file — never committed, never shipped in a build.
- Every engine formula (opinion drift, faction power, cohesion) gets a GUT test with hand-computed expected values before UI work uses it.
- Build order is PRD §6, strictly: briefings/suggestions → opinion drift → debates → recruitment → factions to stage 3 → chapter framework → faction stages 4–6 (Tomas's faction only, data-capped) → raid defense → chapter-1 event chains → art integration. The MVP is a complete Chapter 1 (PRD v2.0, §7); succession/assassination/aging and stages 4–6 for other factions remain post-MVP.
