# Crown and Council

Text-first, LLM-narrated grand strategy game: rule a kingdom for ~60 years through a council of ministers with real personalities, ideologies, and grudges. Godot 4 project whose campaign map is the **3D map** (Terrain3D, `scripts/map3d/`, built by `tools/map3d/` — see its README); every layer draws on it, and it is the ONLY map: the 2D `WorldMapView` was deleted on 2026-09-24, so a checkout with no built world shows the 3D view's notice naming `build_pack.sh`.

## Key documents

- `docs/design/PRD.md` — scope of the first playable build. Build order lives here; follow it.
- `docs/design/COUNCIL_SYSTEM.md` — the council design doc; source of truth for mechanics and formulas.
- `.claude/skills/visual-review/` — the loop for EVERY visual change (shoot, compare variants, crop, independent critic) and `taste.md`, the owner's art rulings as principles; add each new ruling there.
- `tools/NEW_MAP.md` — the checklist and order for bringing in a new map pack. `tools/map3d/README.md` — the Terrain3D pipeline. `assets/flux/README.md` — the Flux art pipeline.
- `docs/design/MANOR_SYSTEM.md` — the manor system (owner 2026-09-13):
  - **Manors and houses.** Every land cell of the pack is a **manor** with a proper name (`political/manors.csv`, forged by `NameForge` / `ManorNames` from the export's name banks; `manor_name_overrides.csv` is hand-maintained) held by a **house** (a family name; the crown is the player character's live family; an unmoved manor belongs to its province's house, so `GameState.manor_owner` holds only what moved and `MapManors.set_owner` needs a reason — `manor_log`). People are never stored and are WHOLE: a province's ledger population is apportioned to its manors by area × land value (largest remainder, `MapManors.people_table`), so manors always sum EXACTLY to their province.
  - **The economy is `ManorEconomy` and `RealmEconomy`, not `MapManors`**: `ManorEconomy.line_of` is one manor's MONTH — whole people, whole-gold tax (its town's tariff included), the units of goods it ships and their worth in the strategic and civil pools, THE ONLY ROUNDING IN THE ECONOMY (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-14-economy-and-staging-design.md`) — and `RealmEconomy` sums those lines into provinces and the realm (columnar, a key per province standing in for a dirty flag, the month's work spread over balanced day buckets; `line_of` / `lines_of_province` / `lines_table` / `tax_table` read the cache, one province key check per bulk read, never one per row), settled at the month's end into `metrics.treasury`, `metrics.strategic` and `metrics.grain` — the CIVIL pool, whose key stays `grain` for its readers. Buildings (`data/manor_buildings.csv`, priced in civil goods by `civil_cost`; `cost_gold` is unused), defence, value (whole people and the month's units, `MapManors.value_of_line`) and the holding house's asking price (marked up by its councillor's opinion) are `MapManors`, every number `balance.csv` `manor_*`.
  - **Orders** `MANOR_BUY` / `MANOR_SELL` / `MANOR_BUILD` / `MANOR_SEIZE` (a skeleton gated by `manor_seize_enabled`, now 0) resolve a manor by code, label or id (`MapManors.find`) and run from the manor card's buttons through `Game.try_execute`. `world_cell_index.png` (importer / `tools/bake_manors.gd`) is how a click finds its manor — only in the MANORS layer; on the political map land still means province.
  - **The MANORS tab** colours manors by house on the 3D ground (`ManorPalette`; see the 3D map below) and puts the **Manor Master** table in the left column (a row centres the map on its manor, activating it opens the card). Each manor without a town is an **estate** by its INDUSTRY and its people (owner 2026-09-14): `data/manor_estates.csv` gives each industry's three `min_people` (the pack's manor-population terciles), `MapObjects.estate_tier(repo, focus, people)` picks the row (an industry with no rows wears the first industry's), the manor card's "Estate" field names industry and size and its painting is the row's `painting`. The 2D map's estate DRAWINGS (library `D-43`..`D-78`) went with the 2D map on 2026-09-24; the owner had ruled them off the 3D map too. The earlier procedural farmsteads-and-fields marks were REMOVED at the owner's ruling (scattered and unreasonable): never place separate houses and fields by hash again.
  - **The player is the king of the realm and the lord of ONE province** (owner 2026-09-13): a province is controlled by the house holding the most of its manors (`MapManors.controller_of`, ties to the province's own house), the crown starts controlling the capital's province alone (`house_bindings.csv`), and everything that reads as the crown's economy — the seeded lord's purse, the dashboard's people and provinces, `WorldLedger.belongs_to_player`, the order prompt's "Your provinces" — is `MapManors.controlled_provinces`; the host stays the realm's; BUILD and FOCUS need a controlled province, SEIZE any province of the realm.
  - **Two purses** (owner 2026-09-14, MANOR_SYSTEM.md §5c): the CROWN's `treasury`/`strategic`/`grain` (a crown in the top bar) take `crown_share_permille` of the realm's other provinces and pay the court and the orders' fees; the LORD's `lord_gold`/`lord_strategic`/`lord_civil` (the house's arms) take the controlled provinces in full, the harvest and the buildings, and pay every manor order inside the realm — `RealmEconomy.purses` is the one split.
  - **Buying land** (`MapManors.purchase_kind`): your own province or a realm province bordering one, from the lord's purse; across the border only a manor whose cell touches the realm, from the treasury, annexed (`MapManors.annex`, `GameState.manor_province`, saved) into the bordering realm province with the most tax and held by its house; anything else refused. A manor's province is therefore STATE: `province_of` / `manors_of_province` take the state, and `MapOwnership.province_loops` merges annexed cells into the outlines by edge parity, so borders, seams, wash and `RealmEconomy`'s index follow.
  - **Every manor works one chief good** (`data/manor_focus.csv`, allowed by biome and coast; the default FOLLOWS THE TERRAIN — owner 2026-09-14 — the first row whose `default_on` claims the cell (`coast`, `high`/`peak` by `manor_focus_*_height`, or biome ids) and that the land allows, else the first allowed; `GameState.manor_focus` holds only chosen ones with the tick, ramping in over `transition_ticks`): yield is people × land value × `yield_per_1000` × the good's growth, whole units a MONTH (`ManorEconomy.focus_units`), it counts in the value, and `MapEconomy.output_of_provinces(repo, provinces, state)` sums the same lines the tick settles; `MANOR_FOCUS` runs from the card's `WORK` buttons like the other manor orders — no model call.
  - **Time runs in days** (owner 2026-09-13): `GameState.day` (1..30) under the tick, the top bar's clock (`||` `>` `2x` `4x`, `time_day_seconds` — 8 s a day at 1x since 2026-09-27, owner: 一个游戏日的时间流逝速度需要放缓很多了 — `time_speed_*`) driven only by `GameRoot._process` on the court AND the council stage through `Game.advance_day` — works step each day, the thirtieth day's end is the tick, an event stops the clock and opens the council, and there is no End Turn and no "Wave them on" first (owner 2026-09-14: play is the only switch); no wall-clock below `Game`.
  - **Building takes time and room**: `build_days` per building, works in `GameState.manor_works`, `manor_slots_base` (+`manor_slots_burg_bonus`) slots per manor, `MANOR_DEMOLISH` refunds `manor_demolish_refund` of a building's `civil_cost` to the CIVIL STORES (a build is bought with goods, so goods come back); finished buildings add `*_per_tick` (gold, grain, church, contentment, unrest) as ledger lines after the month's settlement and `produce_good/units` to the manor's output; the card's slot row (`MapDetailPanel._fill_slots`, icons `bld_*` in `resource_icons.csv`) carries a cross per building.
- `docs/design/CHAPTER_0.md` + `game_story/chapter_0/chapter_0_events.md` — **the prologue, which is the tutorial** (owner 2026-09-21). It is a CHAIN on the existing event system, not a second system: 21 beats in `data/chapter_events.csv` / `chapter_event_choices.csv` / `chapter_branches.csv`, prose and titles in the story document (`StorySource.DOCUMENTS` lists both it and `RANDOM_EVENTS.md`; `### C0-NN` headings, same `**Scene:**` contract), outcome lines in `templates.csv` as `event_c0_*`, illustrations as library `V-35`..`V-55` on the wide `[C-EVENT]` canvas installed to `assets/events/<event_id>.png`. Chapter beats are NOT in `data/events.csv` — the random roll samples that pool by weight and a scripted beat must never be sampled — but they ARE in `events_by_id`, so a chain fires them by id; `EventSystem` reads `DataRepository.event_choices_all()`. `GameState.new_campaign` schedules whichever chapter row carries a `start_tick`, so every new reign opens on the first council. Three engine additions carry it and nothing else: `TriggerDsl`'s `never`, `!` negation and `trait_of=<seat or id>:<trait>`; `EventEffects`' `branch:<branch_id>` op, which schedules the first rule of `chapter_branches.csv` whose `requires` holds, at that rule's own delay; and the event banner's title, which now comes from the story document's own heading (`event_titles`) rather than a `templates.csv` key that the content test forbids. The chapter runs ticks 1–11 (spring to early winter), is settled on four metric conditions at the harvest, and calls no model at all.
- `RANDOM_EVENTS.md` (repo root) — random-events system: triggering rules + the event pool. The "Your event seeds" section at the top is the owner's inbox for raw ideas — when new seeds appear there, expand each into a fully spec'd event (trigger, weight, choices with signatures, chain) in the pool below it. **This file is loaded at runtime**, not just design reference: `StorySource` parses each `### EV-NN` entry's `**Scene:**` field and the game displays that prose verbatim. Its formatting is therefore a contract — keep the heading and `**Scene:**` labels intact, and a test asserts all 20 ids still resolve. **No event is ever model-written:** event stories are fixed authored prose, `Voicer.voice_event` has no path to the API, and no event prompt exists. If an event needs different words, write them here.
- `data/*.csv` — game data, loaded at runtime: `balance.csv` (every tuning number), `council_seats`, `character_traits`, `faction_escalation`, `policy_actions_court_additions`, `action_signatures`, `events`, `event_choices`, `world_rules`, `claim_types`, `facts`, `world_schema`, `metric_polarity`, `order_costs`, `political_palette`, `terrain_palette`, `graphics_quality` (the 3D map's performance tiers), `map3d_bake`, `map3d_fields`, `map3d_settlements`, the `*_art.csv` tables, `army_*` and more. **Design values live in these CSVs, never hardcoded in scripts.** Add columns/rows rather than embedding constants. `data/` carries a `.gdignore` at its root (the per-domain ones are now redundant but harmless) so Godot does not import game tables as translations — it was generating ~120 `.translation`/`.csv.import` sidecars nothing loads; `FileAccess` reads the CSVs regardless, and a future export preset must include `data/**` AND `game_story/**` explicitly (2026-09-21: `game_story/` carries a `.gdignore` for the same reason — the chapter drop's CSV was generating `.translation` sidecars — and the same consequence).
  - **Map packs.** `data/maps.csv` has one row per pack (its bitmaps, its data dir, `is_default`); no code, tool or test names a pack (`DataRepository.default_pack_id()`, `tools/packs.py`). The exception to "CSVs are design data" is the pack tables under `data/maps/<id>/`: `map_info.csv`, `realm_bindings.csv`, `house_bindings.csv` and the optional `manor_name_overrides.csv` / `arms_bindings.csv` at the root (the bindings are hand-maintained, never written by the importer: realms bind authored slots to kingdoms, houses bind the court's families to provinces of the player's realm; unbound provinces are held by a house named after their seat) and, by domain, `geography/` (`biomes`, `cells`, `vertices`, `coasts`, `rivers`, `markers`, `routes`), `political/` (`kingdoms`, `provinces`, `burgs`, `manors`, `borders`, `province_borders`, `diplomacy`, `campaigns`), `economy/` (`kingdoms`, `provinces`, `burgs`, `goods`, `markets`, `market_goods`, `production`, `deals` — the Azgaar export's trade flows, 10,815 rows of seller/buyer (burg or market), good, units, price and tax; imported since 2026-09-20 but deliberately NOT loaded into `DataRepository`, since nothing reads it yet and the right index — by buyer, by good, by route — is unknowable until something does), `military/` (`kingdoms`, `regiments`, `unit_types`), `society/` (`cultures`, `religions`, `zones`).
  - The `world_*_index.png` hit-test bitmaps in `assets/maps/<id>/` are **generated** by `tools/import_azgaar.gd` from the Azgaar JSON export there, and the other `world_*.png` fields by the bakes in `tools/NEW_MAP.md` — hand-edits are overwritten on re-import; lasting changes belong in Azgaar. All pack coordinates are the JSON's own (2560×929 for `beria`); nothing is calibrated or scaled at import. The importer needs only the `*Full*.json`; it never reads a PNG. **Every `world_*.png` is DATA, so it imports LOSSLESS and is never re-imported as VRAM compressed** (which scrambles manor ids and bands the sea): two of them are sampled by 3D shaders, which is what makes the editor's "detect 3D" want to recompress them, so `project.godot`'s `[importer_defaults] texture` carries `detect_3d/compress_to: 0` for the WHOLE project (2026-09-20 — it changes no existing `.import`, which each carry every param explicitly, and it matches what the 3D textures already set by hand; a new pack therefore needs nothing done to its bitmaps) and `tests/map3d/test_map3d_look_assets.gd` fails if a pack's bitmap ever loses `compress/mode=0` or that flag.

Deleted with it: `WorldMapView` and its layers (`SeaLayer`, `GroundLayer`, `BiomeLayer`, `CloudLayer`, `MapInfoCard`, `biome_tiles` / `sea_bands` shaders), `MapDashCache`, `MarkAtlas`, `MarkMesh`, the 2D-only functions of `MapObjects` (the bitmap hit test `object_at`, terrain / sea / estate decor, the settlement drawings), `MapTerrain`'s heat ramp and `MapGeometry`'s dashes; the 2D art (`assets/settlements`, `terrain`, `biomes`, `decor`, `markers`, the estate drawings in `assets/manors/` — its `paint/` stays for the card) with their tables (`settlement_art`, `settlement_growth`, `terrain_art`, `biome_art`, `sea_art`, `marker_art`, and `manor_estates.csv`'s `texture` column) and Flux installers/prompts; `tools/snapshot_map` and `bench_map`; and every `map_*` balance key only it read. **What STAYS**: every pack bitmap and its bake (`bake_relief.gd`, `bake_biome_weights.gd`, `bake_sea_distance.gd`, `bake_settlements.gd`, `bake_manors.gd`) — the 3D build (`bake_world.py`, `plant_trees.gd`) and the 3D map (province / cell index, sea distance) read them; `MapTerrain`'s land mesh, which the map detail card's window draws; and the shared pieces the 3D HUD draws with (`CompassLayer`, `ScaleBar`, `MapLettering`, `ManorBadges`, `ArmyCounters`, `MapRuler`, `ManorPalette`). A checkout with no built world shows the 3D view's notice naming `build_pack.sh`. The 2D map's rulings the 3D map keeps: **the pack's cells ARE the ground** — there is no map render PNG (owner 2026-09-11); **a river runs source → mouth**, the pack's own order, clipped to the land at both ends, and **its COLOUR belongs to the sea** (`river` `#7FB8DD`, owner 2026-09-17: a river and its mouth read as ONE water); **the sea is four flat bands by distance from the shore** in SCHEME C (`sea_near` #A8C4B4 → `sea_nearer` #6FA3A0 → `sea_far` #3D7A93 → `sea_deep` #255C7C, `coast_line` #1E4257, `map_sea_band_*_px`, the pack's `world_sea_distance.png`); **roads are worn tracks, never ruled lines** (owner 2026-09-17); **the scale bar** restates itself at every zoom in the pack's `distance_scale` / `distance_unit`, rounded down to the 1-2-5 ladder (`MapGeometry.nice_step`: "200 km", never "187 km"); and **only a clicked object is ringed** — hovering names it and swaps the left column but draws nothing (owner 2026-09-12).

## Stack

- **Godot 4.7 + GDScript**, **Forward+** renderer since 2026-09-24 (owner; was GL Compatibility — every "under GL Compatibility" trap below is history unless re-checked; Forward+ converts `source_color` uniforms to linear and blends in linear, so a colour passed per VERTEX must be turned linear in the shader, as `border_line.gdshader` does) (typed GDScript everywhere: type hints on vars, params, returns).
- **A tick is a MONTH of 30 days** (`Calendar.DAYS_PER_TICK`, 12 ticks a year): the clock runs `GameState.day` 1..30 under the tick, and the month's end is the settlement.
- **GUT** (Godot Unit Test) for tests. The simulation engine must be unit-testable headless.
- **OpenAI API via raw HTTP** (`HTTPRequest` node) — there is no GDScript SDK. Model IDs and prices live in one config table (see PRD §8), not scattered through code.
- **Run:** `/Applications/Godot.app/Contents/MacOS/Godot --path .`
- **Test:** `./run_tests.sh` (runs the full GUT suite headless; the suite must report the **expected error count** — any other count is fatal. That is "Errors 2" while the `gdai-mcp-plugin-godot` GDExtension is installed, whose class-database probing GUT cannot resolve headless, and 0 without it; the script keys off `gdai_mcp_plugin.gdextension`, which the plugin's own `.gitignore` ignores along with `bin/`, so a fresh clone expects 0 until the binaries are copied in from the plugin zip). `run_tests.sh` exports `CC_FORCE_OFFLINE=1` so the suite can never make a live API call even with a real `.env` present.
- **`.env`** (git-ignored, dev-convenience source for `OpenAIClient.load_config()`; `.env.example` documents the keys — `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `LLM_MODEL`/per-tier overrides, `LLM_MAX_OUTPUT_TOKENS`) overlays defaults and is itself overlaid by `user://config.json` (player override); a missing key or `CC_FORCE_OFFLINE=1` fails closed to `offline_mode: true`.
- **`user://config.json`** (git-ignored, read by `OpenAIClient.load_config()`; missing/unreadable/malformed file, or an empty `api_key`, all fail closed to `offline_mode: true`; `model_order` and `model_flagship` are also accepted):

  ```json
    "api_key": "",
    "model_parse": "gpt-5.6-luna",
    "model_voice": "gpt-5.6-terra",
    "offline_mode": true
  }
  ```

## Architecture invariants (do not violate)

1. **The engine decides; the LLM only writes dialogue.** Concern scans, advisor suggestions, opinion drift, faction power, and escalation are all deterministic engine code. A minister's suggestion is a real `action_code` with real params — always legal, always affordable. Never let the model invent actions. The model may also propose **world deltas** (owner ruling 2026-09-09), but only on paths listed in `data/world_schema.csv`, each clamped to that path's `llm_cap`, each carrying a reason, and only on the player's own realm; the engine still picks the speaker and applies everything through `WorldLedger`.
2. **Determinism.** One seeded RNG stream owned by the engine. No `Time.get_*`, no wall-clock, no unseeded `randi()` inside simulation code. Identical save + identical inputs ⇒ identical state. LLM output enters the engine only as validated action codes, clamped enums (e.g. `tone` deltas capped at ±3), and schema-listed world deltas clamped to their caps.
3. **Simulation core is engine-pure.** Keep sim classes as `RefCounted` (no Node/scene dependencies) so GUT can run them headless. UI observes state via signals; it never mutates it directly.
4. **Readable causality.** Every opinion change carries a reason string at creation time (`−8: revoked noble tax exemption (opposes, centralism)`). Never write an opinion delta without one.
5. **Authored prose never names a kingdom.** The map is a generated Azgaar
   export, so story text, fact text, role names and templates say
   `{player_realm}` / `{raider_realm}` / `{neighbor_realm}` / `{empire_realm}`;
   `DataRepository.fill_realms()` substitutes the bound kingdom's label at
   load. Structural columns keep using the slot itself (`resolve_realm`).
   A re-export that renames the world should touch
   `data/maps/<id>/realm_bindings.csv` and nothing else — tests included:
   fixtures derive their kingdom, capital and roads from the pack, never
   from a literal name.
6. **Territory is state, not pack data.** The map pack ships the *starting*
   border; who holds a province now lives in `GameState.province_owner`
   (only what has moved) and moves through `MapOwnership.set_owner`, which
   requires a reason like every other state change. A kingdom's live outline
   is the merge of the loops of the provinces it holds
   (`province_borders.csv`), so conquest redraws the border, recentres the
   label and repaints the land with no new art. Hit-testing reads the
   province index bitmap — geometry, which never goes stale — and looks the
   owner up; never trust `world_state_index.png` for ownership.
7. **Save = one JSON file.** All state in memory, serialized whole. No database, no partial saves.
8. **Every order carries a cost.** `OrderCosts` guarantees an explicit (gold, grain, population) or hidden (opinion, relationship, attribute) cost per executed order from `data/order_costs.csv` (polarity per metric in `data/metric_polarity.csv`); `COUNCIL_CONSULT` and `EVENT_CHOICE` are the listed exemptions. A typed order is ONE model call returning `{narrative, impacts}`; the screens print the narrative and then the ledger lines that actually applied, so a narrative that overreaches is visible as such.
9. **Cost discipline.** Clicked suggestions bypass the LLM entirely. Low-stakes minister lines use trait-tinted templates with no API call. Reserve the flagship model for rare confrontation/betrayal scenes only.
10. **The columns divide by kind** (owner ruling 2026-09-10). The LEFT
    column is FACTS: the realm dashboard, the country under the cursor, the
    host while the military layer is on, and the fact card of whatever the
    player clicks on the map — clicking an object replaces the left column,
    never the right. The RIGHT slot is PEOPLE: the council column — where
    you stand, who is present, MOVE, and the full record of what was said
    (`ScreenLayout.TALK_WIDTH` on the conversation stage). The TALK itself
    is the **audience window** (owner 2026-09-15, after Crusader Kings III,
    replacing the dossier over the talk in the right column of 2026-09-10):
    centred over the map (`%AudienceLayer` lies exactly on the map panel,
    faintly dimming it), the person on the left — portrait cut head to
    waist before the room (`AudienceBackdrop`: the location kind's tint, or
    a `data/locations.csv` `backdrop` painting), name plate, seat, opinion
    whose tooltip lists its last reasons (`CharacterPortrait.opinion_summary`),
    trait chips with their notes, CK3's row of five skill badges — and on
    the right this beat's exchange (`speech_text`; the record keeps every
    beat once it has passed — while the window shows a beat, it is the
    window's, owner 2026-09-28), BIO / SKILLS / TIES pages that take the speech's place and hand
    it back, the options (their trade-off and gold cost on hover) and the
    talk box. Events and the opening narration stand in the SAME window
    (`event_popup()` is it); only the MOVE list keeps the small popup. So a
    map click never displaces the talk, and the map only narrows for the
    council column.
    The left column's width is fixed
    (`ScreenLayout.LEFT_WIDTH`, 240 design px): anything put in it must wrap
    — a label that cannot wrap sets a minimum width and pushes the column
    over the map — and scroll rather than grow. Each screen owns one
    `_refresh_left()` that applies the precedence (card > hovered country >
    military > the column's own content); no panel hides its own siblings.
11. **The economy is whole gold, rounded once, at the manor.**
    Provinces and the realm are sums; growth is an integer per-mille
    table; iteration order is fixed.

## Conventions

- GDScript style: `snake_case` files/functions/vars, `PascalCase` classes, signals named in past tense (`opinion_changed`).
- The API key is read from a local git-ignored config file — never committed, never shipped in a build.
- Every engine formula (opinion drift, faction power, cohesion) gets a GUT test with hand-computed expected values before UI work uses it.
- **The 3D map look route runs FIRST** (owner 2026-09-24, "美术也很重要"): `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-map3d-humankind-look-gap.md` — Forward+ renderer, oblique camera, cliffs and rock, ground and colour, natural sea near / SCHEME C bands far, settlements and overlays, phase by phase; trees DEFERRED (the "no trees" ruling stands until the owner rules). It is taken ahead of the gameplay steps below. Done so far (overnight 2026-09-24/25, the spec's §4b): Forward+ with per-tier SSAO/SSIL/haze (`graphics_quality.csv`), a tilted camera, AgX plus a warm per-channel curve (`map3d_env_*`), the sea natural near and banded far (`map3d_sea_natural_full_m` / `_bands_full_m`, `map3d_sea_nat_*`), escarpments baked into the high ground (`bake_world.terrace`, `terrace_*`), a northwest sun, small irregular fields, and a parchment halo under the kingdom names.
- Build order is PRD §6, strictly (reaffirmed by the owner 2026-09-18): briefings/suggestions → opinion drift → debates → recruitment → factions to stage 3 → chapter framework → faction stages 4–6 (Tomas's faction only, data-capped) → raid defense → chapter-1 event chains → art integration. The MVP is a complete Chapter 1 (PRD v2.0, §7) — every feature, all its art, all its gameplay, nothing placeholder (owner 2026-09-18); succession/assassination/aging and stages 4–6 for other factions remain post-MVP.
