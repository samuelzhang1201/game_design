# The owner's taste

High-level aesthetic principles distilled from the owner's rulings. Each is a
judgement about what looks right and why — never how to build it. Numbers,
keys, techniques and the history of a ruling live in the subsystem's
`CLAUDE.md`, its guide, or the feature's spec. When a new ruling lands, add
the principle it teaches here in one or two sentences, or sharpen an
existing one.

## The world is one picture
- **One medium.** Everything on the map belongs to the same photograph, lit by
  the same sun. Anything drawn in another medium reads as a sticker.
- **Nothing ends on a hard cut.** Places, textures, water and marks fade into
  what surrounds them. A tray, slab, disc, ring or base under an object is the
  most common reason something is called ugly.
- **Objects stand on the world's own ground.** No base plates on the map —
  ownership is told by marks, not by pedestals.
- **Material and life rescue a picture; a colour grade cannot.** When the
  ground looks fake, the fix is in what it is made of and what lives on it,
  not in tinting it.
- **Water is one water.** A river becomes the sea it meets, without a seam,
  and every shore follows the land, never a grid.
- **Each material is seen from where the player looks.** Art made at another
  angle or scale reads false from above.
- **Nature clusters.** Woods, ranges, fields and towns gather and follow a
  logic; an even scatter reads as random and unreasonable.
- **Places are systems, not decorations.** A town grows round its centre with
  lanes and open space, fields belong to a settlement, a wall closes, a
  harbour belongs to its town.
- **The ground remembers, briefly.** What crosses the land leaves a mark that
  answers the ground it lies on, then fades.

## Realism, not toy
- **Simple realism.** Buildings, figures and props are sober, weathered and
  truthfully proportioned — never cartoon, never a "game asset", no flags or
  ornaments added for charm.
- **Hand-built, not machined.** Stone and timber are irregular; a perfectly
  straight edge or a clean wall reads as CG.
- **Worn, not new.** Kit is field kit, paper is old, walls are stained at the
  foot.
- **Physically possible.** A building stands plumb on its ground; a body only
  holds poses a body can make, from every side.
- **Colour a little richer than true, never lighter.** Warmth and variety stay
  inside one palette; a shade that turns pink, chalky or garish is outside it.

## Readability at the scale it is played
- **Judge at the zoom the game is played at.** A detail, a motion or a mark
  that only reads in a close-up does not exist for the player; check the
  nearest and the farthest zoom.
- **At small sizes, silhouette and colour carry meaning — not detail.** One
  big legible sign beats many small ones.
- **Legibility may overrule real scale** (roads, rivers, houses, pieces), but
  never so far that one thing reads as a different kind of thing, or dwarfs
  its neighbours.
- **Pieces are miniatures, not monuments.** They grow and shrink with the
  table, findable but never the biggest thing on the map.
- **Kinds of the same family part at a glance**, even with the clock paused.
- **Motion must be seen, and be the right motion.** Wind is a travelling
  crest, not a drifting veil; a shimmer too small to see is stillness.
- **A walking figure's feet hold the ground, and a group never moves as
  clones.** A foot that glides reads as floating, at every clock speed; men
  in one file step out of time with each other — the variety comes from the
  men themselves (height, stride, phase), never from feet that slip.
- **Sharp up close, clean from far, at any window size.**

## Light and atmosphere
- **The sun models the land; it never hides it.** Shadows stay light and
  short enough to read the map through.
- **Time of day is a light, never a filter.** Dusk warms the sun, not the
  whole picture.
- **Depth is blue air, never grey fog.**
- **The sky is sparse, faint and even** over the whole world.
- **Weather is a patch, not a blanket.** The look may be smaller than the rule.

## Lines and marks
- **Lines rank by pattern and weight, not by colour alone.** A tool never
  borrows the map's line language.
- **A mark on the map is ink, not a badge.**
- **The object is its own label.** Nothing floats over a piece on the map —
  no counters, numbers or bars; what it is and how it fares live in its card.
  Lettering shows only at the zoom where it helps.
- **Pieces behave like objects in the world, not billboards.** They keep the
  heading they last moved on; the camera never turns them.
- **A selection strengthens what is already there** instead of laying a new
  colour over it.
- **Washes are tinctures, not highlighters** — the ground must read through.
- **A failure is drawn where it fails, not hidden.** A line that no longer
  works stays on the map in a still, dark, broken form that stops where it
  breaks — never the working line's colour, never simply gone.

## The table and its objects
- **The play screens are a war table by daylight.** Every element is a real
  object lying on it — paper, wood, brass, wax — under one light. What is held
  up to the eye (windows, events, menus, the council) is dark leather.
- **Material over ornament.** One ornament per layer; everything else is
  material and light. A frame inside a frame is noise.
- **Things arrive the way their object would** — a sheet is laid down, a
  scroll unrolls; nothing simply cuts in.
- **Every surface is a real material**, aged and lit, never a flat fill.
- **Every element is a crafted object, never a placeholder.** A flat disc or
  a bare line reads unfinished; even an abstract view is made of things —
  and what connects stays finer than what it connects.
- **Heraldic colour, not dashboard colour.** One muted family of tinctures,
  muted only as far as the distinctions it carries survive.

## Words and information
- **Read in a book face; shout only in titles.** Body text is lowercase and
  readable; display faces are kept for names and titles.
- **A warning speaks in the page's own rubric, at the size of the facts round
  it** — never a pale tint that reads quieter than the plain ink beside it.
- **Name every reading.** Art + name + value; the icon anchors the word, never
  replaces it. Never truncate, never make the player hover to understand.
- **Commands look like commands, speech like speech.**
- **One primary action per screen, controls grouped by kind.**
- **What the eye already knows needs no caption.**
- **The game speaks in its own voice**, even when something is empty.
- **The map is the subject.** Interface pieces sit at its edges, small and
  ordered, never over what the player is looking at.

## How the owner judges
- Show two or three options side by side and let the owner choose; never
  queue new art without asking.
- The owner judges real shots at the playing zooms, and names a zoom by the
  scale bar.
- A ruling stands until the owner reverses it: do not bring back what was
  removed without being asked.

## Screens and menus
- **The title is a film still, not a menu page.** The living world fills the
  screen; the words sit low in a darkened corner, light and few, and the one
  way in is the brightest thing on them. The game opens from black onto that
  picture, never onto a placeholder that is then swapped.
