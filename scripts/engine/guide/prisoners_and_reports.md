# Prisoner disposition and delivered battle records

## Negotiated prisoner disposition

`PrisonerDisposition.propose` saves immutable kind, selections, price, parties and optional noncombat deadline without paying or releasing. `respond` records each required realm's one decision; rejection is terminal. Unconditional release needs the holding realm, ransom needs the holder and original payer, exchange needs reciprocal holding realms, and noncombat terms also need the released realm. `settle` revalidates all selections/laws/funds and publishes the candidate transaction together, including both exchange sides, personnel, treasury entries, unit prizes, promises and family feedback. Exact saved retries return the receipt; conflicting IDs fail.

`PRISONER_PROPOSE`, `PRISONER_RESPOND` and `PRISONER_SETTLE` derive authority from the player realm and charge their `order_costs` clerk fee once on successful commands. Clerk fees and any ordinary postage commit atomically with successful issuance; remote execution later retains those issuance fees even if fulfillment fails. Neither prospective income nor reserved battlefield rewards can pay an unaffordable fee. Complete action receipts replay before fees or history. These commands offer no escort, capital deposit or automatic return to service. Captured `ransom_protected` preserves accepted ransom protection after the battlefield is removed; a later received execution policy cannot revoke it. Shares read delivered military law, never an undelivered decree. Kind labels, family gratitude and reasons live in `battle/prisoner_terms.csv`; gratitude credits the actual releasing realm. The visible cards and remote report delivery belong to the presentation layer.

A ransom proposal also freezes an informational price reference: selected noble count times `prisoner_terms` base price plus named wealth (or the unnamed wealth assumption) times its per-point price, capped separately by the payer's current unreserved capacity. The agreed price remains a negotiated whole-gold amount; current affordability is checked again at settlement. These valuation inputs and initial share are provisional tuning.

## Delivered battle records and custody cards

`BattleReport` keeps per-unit entry identity and the saved daily journal, then freezes one terminal snapshot after winner and warrant closure. Participants and their supply lines are ordered by side and unit code so JSON reload cannot change the report. Public details come only from `for_realm`: own personnel exact, enemy losses estimated, own commanders/formations/orders/events and directly known surrendered equipment. A called-off town battle emits its terminal result through the same report route. `BattleReport.record.custody` freezes original captures plus batch IDs affected by later `battle_holder:<battle_id>:*` receipts, preserving each original `battle_id`; terminal reports include those dated prior-holder observations. `BattleReport.leave` freezes `exit_supplies` and rejoining clears it, excluding consumption after departure from that battle’s accounting. Battle-linked spoils carry their battle ID and separate settlement receipt; they never reopen the frozen personnel record or bypass remote delivery.

`MissiveWriter.king_report_payload` freezes safe details, own unit readings and custody observations before dispatch. Local observation and physical courier arrival attach identical safe payload fields to their battle missive. `BattlePresentation` reads received payloads only; army cards use its pending-report gate to avoid exposing remote live losses. Custody displays its observation day and whether verified locally; later undisclosed transfers cannot alter a dated remote observation. Fulfilled royal disposition receipts update known released people once by cumulative released totals, preserving immutable capture history. Proposal, consent and fulfillment remain distinct. Named links inspect the original character record without moving the player or starting dialogue. Prisoners and wounded remain personnel/supply/carry accounts; the approved unit-behavior design §6 forbids their 3D models and additional groups.

## Physical agreement letters

`PrisonerCommunications` carries production proposals to each foreign party's held bound capital by an ordinary paid rider. The foreign decision is made on arrival: lawful ransom within the frozen asking reference and current funds accepts; lawful reciprocal exchanges require equal counts; lawful releases and noncombat promises accept. The same rider brings that decision to the king's current position; only receipt calls `PrisonerDisposition.respond`. Same-day unstarted letters to the same recipient and phase share one rider and postage. Letters never alter military order IDs or authorize ordinary orders to foreign armies.

Fulfillment validates a complete candidate before issuance. When every frozen holder is within royal command range it settles immediately; otherwise local holders count as delivered and ordinary command riders follow the remaining holders, including treaty-authorized foreign holders. Nobody releases until the final command arrives and the atomic settlement revalidates all frozen custody, laws and funds. That rider returns the frozen success or failure receipt to the moving king. Issuance fees remain paid on later failure; execution charges no extra fee. Each new leg starts at the next day-end. Saved `prisoner_proposals[id].communications` holds offer phases and received decisions, the fulfillment's sorted holders/delivered/rider IDs, and received visible status/result. `projection` is the public gate: execution alone discloses no result. A lost return leg exposes only an unconfirmed status and neutral overdue reason, never the undelivered decision or outcome; a new agreement is required. Legacy/direct proposals without metadata retain immediate visibility.

## Family losses (`FamilyLosses`)

`FamilyLosses.consume(repo, state, loss_id, changes, context)` turns noble casualties into opinion moves. `changes` are `MilitaryRoster` transition rows `{cohort_id, from, to, count}`; only cohorts of identity `noble` with a family name moving `fit`/`wounded` to `dead` or `captured_current` count (wounded and commoners never do). Heads are grouped by family, cause (`dead`, `captured`) and kind (`named` when the cohort has a `person_id`, else `cadre`); every Character of that family in the PLAYER realm, in id order, takes `heads x family_loss_opinion_<cause>_<kind>` (battle `balance.csv`) with a reason naming family, count, cause, `context.place` and `context.order`. Returns the applied rows `{family, person, delta, reason}`. The receipt `military_events["family:" + loss_id]` holds `{request, result}`: the same request replays the saved rows and applies nothing, a different request under the id returns `{error}`. A dead named commander's Character is not marked dead here.
