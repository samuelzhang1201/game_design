# Settlements and houses

- **Settlements are real house clusters on the ground**, placed by the bake (`tools/map3d/bake_houses.py`, from `sow_patchwork` where the land mask, population and footprint live, so "never in the sea" is structural and a test asserts it): lanes grown from the centre and houses packed along them — compact and messy, medieval, with negative space, never an even scatter; the landmark building at the centre on a levelled terrace with an open square ringed by houses facing it; every group's count within 5:1. `bake/houses.csv` holds world metres with no `y`.
- `Map3DHouses` / `Map3DLayerHouses` draw one MultiMesh per house TYPE, cut once per camera pose, nothing past `map3d_house_view_m`, capped by the tier's `house_budget`.
- **A house stands UPRIGHT on the HIGHEST ground under its footprint and nothing of it is ever below the ground**: `Map3DHouses.ground_under` samples a grid over the whole mesh AABB, `seat` is its highest plus a hair; every mesh carries its own stone footing traced from its walls (never a box pedestal); the bake keeps houses off ground that would need a tall one (`house_max_drop_m`, `house_landmark_max_drop_m`).
- **Houses are drawn at TWICE life size** so a settlement reads as houses, not gravel; `width_m` / `depth_m` / `height_m` in `data/screens/map3d/map3d_houses.csv` are MEASURED from the built meshes (`scale_houses.py`), and the packer reserves each building's real rectangle. A settlement's tier reads from what stands at its centre and townhouses against cottages, never a tenfold range in roof count.
- **Walls, piers and ships**: a settlement whose `features` name `walls` is ringed by a CLOSED wall — its line kept back from the sea, a river's near bank and a cliff's edge (`wall_shore_m`, `wall_river_pull_m`, `wall_max_slope`), never a module left out — with gates where roads cross, a water gate where it crosses a river and a quay gate at its pier, and towers only at sharp turns and every few hundred metres, spaced (`wall_tower_*`). How each type stands is its table's `seat` (`Map3DHouses.seat_of`): a house its highest ground; a wall module `slope` — on its centre's ground, SHEARED along its length onto the ground's slope between its ends so the wall runs on continuously over the hill, its own masonry running 8 m down, never a house footing; a tower or gate `ground`; a pier `sea` (deck `map3d_pier_deck_m` over it); a ship `water` (its waterline on the sea, rocking on the swell in `house_surface.gdshader` `bob_*`, set in the ship's scene). A port's pier stands on the shore NEAREST ITS HOUSES — the timber `pier` off a low shore, the `pier_cliff` (its own rock, stair and castle, at its own proportions) off a capital's cliff, the small landing `pier_stair` (a jetty and a stair up its rock to a gate) off the cliff of a town on a cliff top (`pier_stair_town_rise_m`), each walked out to sea until no taller cliff buries its head (`pier_cliff_*`, `pier_stair_*`, `bake_houses._HEADS`) — and 1-2 ships by its size lie alongside it (`ship_*`).
- **Each house type is a SCENE**: `scenes/models/buildings/<type>.tscn` inherits its `.glb` and gives every surface its `house_surface.gdshader` material in the editor — texture, roughness, vertex colour, normal map and `role` (1 wall, 2 tile roof, 0 untinted) — so opened there it is the house as the map draws it. `house_models.tres` names the scenes, `Map3DAssetCache.house_model` reads their mesh and materials, and `Map3DHouseMaterials.tinted` only COPIES them, adding the Houses node's tints (a material with no tints, `wall_count` 0, draws as built) and the weather — still one MultiMesh per type, never a node per house. The textures are the files the glTF importer extracts beside each `.glb` (`gltf/embedded_image_handling=1`), imported as Basis Universal (`compress/mode=4`) and COMMITTED; `test_map3d_house_scenes.gd` holds all of it.
- **Houses vary by palette, stretch and mix, never by new models.** A settlement wears ONE wall palette and ONE roof palette (`house_wall_palettes` x `house_wall_palette_size`, the roofs' likewise, in `map3d_bake.csv`; the Houses node's `wall_tints` / `roof_tints` are exactly those palettes, a test asserts the counts), picked by a hash of its code so towns differ; the bake writes each house's `wall` / `roof` index (`bake_houses.assign_variants`), no two houses of the same type, wall and roof within `house_variant_clear_m` while another pair is free, and the runtime uses the baked index (`Map3DHouses.variant_of`; a blank cell hashes the position as before). `trim_tints` (custom data b, hashed) tints the untinted surfaces (timber, slate). A type's `stretch` (`map3d_houses.csv`) widens a house along its own x within that share (the packer reserves the stretched rectangle; never a negative scale); `rim_mul` weights a type at the cluster's edge past `house_rim_frac`, `stray_weight` lets a type outside the group's `min_group` stray into its core. Roofs are tile or slate in varied colours, never thatch, never a dark brown tile. **No base plates** on any house mesh. Building style is SIMPLE REALISM, never toy. How the meshes are made: `tools/CLAUDE.md`.
- **Every building wears an APRON** (`Map3DHouseApron`, `house_apron.gdshader`, `data/screens/map3d/map3d_aprons.csv`): one flat quad per house, scaled to its mesh footprint plus `margin_m`, `lift_m` over its seat, its edge fading into the ground over `fade_m`, in ONE MultiMesh per kind drawn with the houses (same `map3d_lod_houses_m` gate, no shadow). The kind is the settlement group's `map3d_settlements.apron` (`dirt` packed earth for a village, `brick` paving for a fort, town, city, capital); a type wears one when `map3d_houses.apron` is 1 (walls, piers, ships do not). The materials are built from the kind's row (colours, `style`, `unit_m`).
