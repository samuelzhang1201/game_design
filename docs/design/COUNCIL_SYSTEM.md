> **Not yet built:** scaffolding decay, council debates, recruitment,

> is a **MONTH of 30 days** (`Calendar.DAYS_PER_TICK`, 12 ticks a year); the
> clock runs day by day under play/pause and there is no End Turn (owner
> 2026-09-14).

## The loop inverts

Right now the loop is: player types → parse → execute → narrate. Adding the council makes it:

```
the clock runs day by day; the 30th day's end is the tick (the month's settlement)
  → [ENGINE] concern scan: which metrics crossed a threshold in whose domain
  → [ENGINE] advisor heuristics generate concrete suggested actions
  → [ENGINE] event roll — an event stops the clock and opens the council
  → [TEMPLATES] ministers voice their concerns and pitch their suggestions
       (trait-tinted lines from data/templates.csv, no API call)
  → PLAYER responds in the audience window — click a suggestion, or type freely
  → [LLM] a typed order is ONE call returning {narrative, impacts}
  → [ENGINE] validate + execute + court reaction pass; the screen prints the
       narrative, then the ledger lines that actually applied
```

(Superseded 2026-09-09..14: the batched LLM briefing and the separate
parse → narrate calls. `Voicer.voice_briefing` and `Voicer.converse` still
exist but no screen calls them; the briefing is template-voiced.)

The important architectural point: **the engine decides who speaks and what they propose. The LLM only writes the dialogue.** A minister's suggestion is a real `action_code` with real params, generated by `SuggestionGenerator`: each scanned concern maps to fixed candidate actions (`ConcernScanner` thresholds are `balance.csv` `concern_*`), each validated by `ActionSystem` before it is offered — the utility heuristics the AI opponents were to share are not built. It is always legal, always affordable, always mechanically sound. If you let the model invent suggestions it will propose things the engine can't do, which is the fastest way to break player trust in advisors.

## Why this fixes the blank-page problem

A free text box with no prompt is intimidating and produces bad input. A chancellor saying *"The salt duty is bringing in a third less than last year — merchants are routing around the coast road to avoid it. I'd cut it by four points and make it back on volume. Or we hold firm and I find the money elsewhere. Your call."* produces good input, because it teaches the player the vocabulary, the causal model, and the available levers all at once.

Suggestions are also **clickable, which means zero LLM parse cost**. Early-game players who mostly click are cheap to serve. That's a nice alignment between tutorial design and unit economics.

## Scaffolding decay

*Not yet built.* Today every month's concerns produce up to three
suggestions (concern-driven first, then a standing menu filler), with no
phase change over the years.

| Phase | Ticks | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **Guided** | Year 1 (months 1–12) | Chancellor opens every tick. 2–3 suggestions with explicit numbers and a plain-language explanation of the trade-off. After execution, explains what moved and why. |
| **Assisted** | Years 2–5 | Ministers raise concerns but don't pre-fill parameters. "Grain stores are thin going into winter." Player decides the response. |
| **Independent** | Year 5+ | Ministers speak only on threshold breaches, on disagreement, and when asked via `COUNCIL_CONSULT`. |

Player-overridable in settings. Some people will want Guided forever; let them.

## Character model

Three separate layers. Keep them separate — conflating them is what makes advisor systems feel like flavour text.

**1. Attributes (0–100)** — who they are

`ambition` `loyalty` `integrity` `boldness` `piety` `compassion` `vanity` `temper` `pragmatism`

(Every role row in `data/founding_council.csv` / `data/roles.csv` also
carries `health` `wealth` `prestige` `influence` (0–100) and `age`.)

**2. Skills (0–20)** — what they're good at

`sk_admin` `sk_martial` `sk_intrigue` `sk_diplomacy` `sk_steward`

**3. Ideology (−100…+100 on each axis)** — what they *want*

| Axis | −100 | +100 |
|---|---|---|
| `centralism` | Feudal privilege | Royal absolutism |
| `militarism` | Mercantile | Martial |
| `orthodoxy` | Tolerance | Religious rigour |
| `tax_incidence` | Burden the commons | Burden the nobles |
| `expansionism` | Consolidate | Conquer |

Traits (see `character_traits.csv`) apply modifiers to attributes and skills at build time. (The trait rows carry no ideology columns, so the designed nudge to starting ideology is not built; ideology comes straight from each role's row.)

## The mechanic that makes arguments real

**Opinion drift is computed from policy-position distance, not from LLM judgment.**

Every action in the taxonomy carries an ideology signature (`data/action_signatures.csv`, one row per action code and variant). Decreeing the church law to `tithe` (`LAW_SET`, variant `law=church_policy&value=tithe`; a range law's variant names its direction, `law=tax_rate&delta_pos`) scores `centralism +10, orthodoxy −50`; `MANOR_SEIZE` scores `centralism +30, militarism +40`. A decree sent again, at the same value, to the places its couriers missed is no change of law: it drifts no one. Event choices carry the same five columns in `data/event_choices.csv`. When you execute an action (`OpinionDrift.apply`, every seated minister):

```
alignment     = Σ(sig_axis × ideology_axis) / (Σ|sig_axis| × 100)     # −1..+1
opinion_delta = round(alignment × policy_sensitivity/100
                      × (1 − pragmatism/100) × opinion_drift_scale)
```

`opinion_drift_scale` is 20 (`balance.csv`). A zero delta writes nothing; every other delta carries its reason — `<label> (approves|opposes, <dominant axis>)`.

A pious Chaplain with `orthodoxy +80` loses opinion every time you tax the clergy — deterministically, predictably, and for a reason the player can understand and plan around. A pragmatic one barely notices.

This is what generates genuine disagreement without the LLM deciding anything. It also means **council conflict is fully reproducible in a save**, which matters for your determinism requirement.

*Not yet built:* two ministers with opposing signs on the same axis and both high `temper` produce a **council debate**: the engine detects the clash, both voice their position, and the player rules via `COUNCIL_DEBATE_RULE`. Picking a side swings opinion both ways. Refusing to pick costs a little with both — realistic, and prevents debate-dodging as a free strategy.

## How player prompts change people

The order call's `impacts` carries a `court_reaction` block alongside `actions` (and schema-listed `world` deltas):

```json
"court_reaction": {
  "addressed": ["char_chancellor"],
  "tone": "curt",
  "promises": [{"character_id":"char_chancellor","promise_code":"CHARACTER_REWARD","tick_due":48}]
}
```

`tone` is an enum — `respectful` `neutral` `curt` `flattering` `threatening` `dismissive` — with **small clamped opinion deltas** (`ActionSystem.TONE_DELTAS`: +2 / 0 / −2 / +2 / −3 / −3, capped by `tone_delta_cap` 3). Deliberately small. Being nice in the text box should not substitute for actually giving people what they want.

`promises` is the interesting one. If the player says "support me on this and I'll see you rewarded," the order call logs it with a deadline. A `promise_code` must be a real action code (`ResponseValidator` refuses anything else); a missing `tick_due` defaults to `promise_default_ticks` (12 — a year of months). Kept promises give `promise_kept_opinion` +15. **Broken promises are the single largest opinion penalty in the system** (`promise_broken_opinion` −25) and mark the character with a permanent `grudge` flag — the hook for faction affinity once factions exist. This is how you get emergent betrayal narratives without scripting them.

## Relationships

Directed graph. **Built:** `GameState.relationships`, edges `{from, to, type, sentiment, baseline}` seeded from `data/relationships.csv` (`from_id,to_id,rel_type,sentiment,reason`). Types in use: `kin` `marriage` `ally` `rival` `grudge` `liege` `admires` `puppet` `patron`, plus `acquaintance` for an edge first created by a shock. Event choices shock an edge with the `rel:` effect op (clamped ±100, with a reason); every month each edge heals `relationship_drift_per_tick` (0.5) toward its founding baseline; `RelationshipGraph.describe_for` feeds the strongest edges into each role's persona prompt. See `role_player.md` §Relationships.

Designed types not yet in the data: `friend` `mentor` `debt` `blood_feud`.

Formation rules, all deterministic — *not yet built*:

- **Rivalry** when ideology distance > 120 AND both `ambition` > 60 AND competing for the same seat
- **Friendship** when ideology distance < 40 AND `temper` compatible AND co-serving > 8 ticks (8 months now that a tick is a month — see open questions)
- **Blood feud** when you execute or imprison someone's kin. Never decays. Inherits at 50% strength to their children.

That last one is the sharpest tool in the box. Executing a troublesome noble solves this turn's problem and creates a lifelong enemy in his son, who will be at your court in twenty years. Over a 60-year campaign this compounds into real history.

## Factions

*Not yet built* — only the data exists (`data/faction_escalation.csv`, the `faction_seed` / `claimant` trait flags, and the `FACTION_NEGOTIATE` / `FACTION_SPLIT` rows in `policy_actions_court_additions.csv`, which `ActionSystem` does not execute yet). PRD §6 Steps 5 and 7 schedule it.

Factions form when the engine detects ≥2 characters sharing a grievance axis, with `loyalty` below threshold and `ambition` above it. A `kin_of_king` or `amb_ruthless` character can seed one alone.

**Faction power** = Σ over members of (`sk_intrigue` + `sk_martial` + personal levy + noble title weight) × cohesion. Cohesion falls as membership grows and ideology spread widens — which is what makes `FACTION_SPLIT` work.

Escalation runs through six stages (`faction_escalation.csv`): grumbling → petition → obstruction → conspiracy → open revolt → deposition. Each has a dwell time (`min_ticks_at_stage`: 4, 6, 8, 10 months for stages 1–4) and a power threshold (`min_faction_power`: 5, 15, 25, 40, 55, 75), so nothing goes from grumbling to civil war in one tick.

**Telegraphing is a hard design rule.** Stage 2 (petition) is always visible and always states the demand explicitly. A player who reaches stage 5 has ignored at least three clear warnings. The exception is stage 4 (conspiracy), which is hidden *without a Spymaster* — that's the mechanical consequence of leaving the seat empty, and it needs to be spelled out in the tutorial.

## Assassination

*Not yet built (post-MVP).* Handle this carefully. Losing a 40-hour campaign to an unforeseeable dice roll is rage-quit material and will show up in your Steam reviews.

Rules:

1. **Only reachable from stage 4.** Never a random event.
2. **Always telegraphed.** With a Spymaster: explicit warnings. Without: ambient signs — a guard reassigned, a dish tasted twice, a courtier absent from council. The information is available; the player has to be paying attention.
3. **Player has counters.** `GUARD_ADJUST`, `CHARACTER_INVESTIGATE`, arrest, preemptive concession.
4. **Success is not instant death.** Roll on a table: failed attempt (conspiracy exposed) → wounded (`maimed`, temporary capacity loss) → incapacitated (regency) → killed. Weight heavily toward the first two.
5. **Death is not necessarily game over.** Succession to your heir is the better outcome — it continues the campaign, adds narrative weight, and turns a punishment into a story. Reserve hard game-over for stage 6 deposition with no heir.

## Ministers age and die

*Not yet built (post-MVP).* They should. Over 60 years you'll go through three generations of council. When a minister dies:

- Their son enters the candidate pool, inheriting traits at each trait's `inherit_chance`
- Inherits relationships at 50% strength, **including blood feuds against you**
- Their faction membership does not transfer, but their grievance does

This is most of what makes a 60-year campaign feel like a dynasty rather than a long spreadsheet.

## LLM cost

The concern scan, suggestion generation, opinion drift, faction power, and escalation are **all deterministic**. The model is OpenAI over raw HTTP (`OpenAIClient`, tiers `model_parse` / `model_order` / `model_voice` / `model_flagship` in config). As built, the LLM is used for:

| Call | Frequency | Batching |
|---|---|---|
| Typed words — an order or plain conversation | Each utterance, capped at `prompt_calls_per_tick` (6) a month | ONE call returning `{narrative, impacts}` (`Game.submit_free_text` → `Voicer.order`, order tier); the engine picks the one speaker before the call, everyone in the audience remembers the words |

**Template-voiced, no API call:** the council briefing — concerns, pitches, promise news, the council summary (`TemplateVoicer`, `data/templates.csv`, trait-tinted). **Never model-written:** event stories, which are the authored `**Scene:**` prose of `RANDOM_EVENTS.md` (`Voicer.voice_event` has no path to the API). Clicked suggestions and event choice buttons cost nothing.

Designed but not built: a debate scene (one call, both sides), faction petition text, and confrontation / betrayal scenes on the flagship tier — the `model_flagship` tier is configured but nothing calls it yet.

## Data tables

The original relational sketch. **As built** there are no such tables at
runtime: a character is a `Character` object (`scripts/engine/character.gd`)
built from `data/founding_council.csv` / `data/roles.csv` (attributes,
skills, ideology and `policy_sensitivity`, traits, opinion with its reason
log, seat, family, kingdom); appointments are `GameState.council`
(seat → id); promises are `GameState.promises`; relationships are
`GameState.relationships`. Factions, conspiracies and the candidate pool do
not exist yet.

```
character(id, session_id, name, dynasty_id, birth_tick, death_tick, gender,
          culture_id, religion_id, home_province, is_player, pool_status)

character_attribute(character_id, ambition, loyalty, integrity, boldness,
                    piety, compassion, vanity, temper, pragmatism)

character_skill(character_id, sk_admin, sk_martial, sk_intrigue,
                sk_diplomacy, sk_steward)

character_ideology(character_id, centralism, militarism, orthodoxy,
                   tax_incidence, expansionism, policy_sensitivity)

character_trait_link(character_id, trait_code, acquired_tick, expires_tick)

character_relation(from_id, to_id, relation_type, opinion, since_tick, decay_rate)

council_appointment(session_id, seat_code, character_id, appointed_tick, ended_tick)

promise(id, character_id, promise_code, made_tick, due_tick, status)

faction(id, session_id, leader_id, grievance_axis, demand_code,
        stage, power, cohesion, formed_tick, last_escalation_tick)

faction_member(faction_id, character_id, commitment, joined_tick)

conspiracy(id, faction_id, target_type, progress, detected, formed_tick)

court_event(id, session_id, tick, event_code, actors_json, outcome_json)
```

All in-memory objects, serialized to the JSON save — same as everything else. No database.

## MVP slice

Don't build all of this at once. Order (the authoritative build order is PRD §6, which extends this list to ten steps and pulls faction stages 4–6 for Tomas's faction into the MVP):

1. **Five founding seats, fixed starting characters, briefing + clickable suggestions.** This alone validates the guidance concept and is the highest-value piece. — *built*
2. **Opinion drift from ideology distance.** Ministers start visibly agreeing and disagreeing. — *built*
3. **Council debates and `COUNCIL_DEBATE_RULE`.** — *not yet built*
4. **Recruitment pool and `RECRUIT_COURTIER`** at year 1. — *not yet built* (the action row and its cost exist; `ActionSystem` does not execute it)
5. **Factions through stage 3 (obstruction).** Stop here for the first playable build. — *not yet built*
6. Stages 4–6, conspiracies, assassination, succession. — *not yet built*

Steps 1–3 give you a council that feels alive. Steps 5–6 give you the drama, but they're only meaningful once players have spent enough hours to have real relationships to betray.

## Risks specific to this system

**Dialogue repetition.** Ministers will say the same things by year 15. Mitigate with trait-tinted template variety, a recent-line memory in the prompt (built for conversation: `AgentMemory` keeps `memory_exchanges_kept` exchanges per character), and by having ministers reference specific recent events rather than generic states.

**Advisor tyranny.** If suggestions are always optimal, players stop thinking and just click. Deliberately make advisors give *ideologically biased* advice rather than optimal advice — a militarist Marshal genuinely over-recommends war. The player's job is to weigh conflicting counsel, not to obey.

**Unreadable causality.** If loyalty drops for reasons the player can't see, the system feels arbitrary. Every opinion change needs a hover-inspectable reason string: "−8: revoked noble tax exemption (opposes, centralism)". Build this from day one, not as polish. (Built: the audience window's opinion tooltip lists the last reasons, `CharacterPortrait.opinion_summary`.)

**Frustration spikes.** Obstruction at stage 3 means orders start failing. Some players will read this as a bug rather than a mechanic. It needs loud, explicit in-fiction signalling.
